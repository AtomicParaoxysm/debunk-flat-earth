Geometry & Formula

Sun-centred: Earth E(t) = 1 AU · (cos 2πt/365.25, sin …), Venus V(t) = a · (cos 2πt/Pᵥ, sin …)

Distance Δ = |V − E|, apparent size θ = 2 · arctan(Rᵥ / Δ), Rᵥ = 6,052 km

Phase angle i at Venus, between the Sun and us: cos i = (s · e) / (|s| |e|)

Lit fraction we see: k = (1 + cos i) / 2 (1 = full, 0 = new)

Earth-centred (Ptolemy): same Venus path around us, but the Sun sits farther out at S, beyond Venus

Sun-centred (Copernicus) Earth-centred (Ptolemy) What Galileo saw in 1610
In plain English: Venus can only look full when the Sun is lighting it from behind, as seen from us. That only happens when Venus is on the far side of the Sun. Telescopes show Venus full exactly when it looks smallest and farthest away, so Venus must be going around the Sun, not around the Earth.
