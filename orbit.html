Geometry & Formula

Measured: every star traces a yearly loop of half-width κ = 20.5″, near or far

If Earth moves at speed v, light arrives tilted by κ = v / c, so v = c · κ

In plain English: Walk through rain and it seems to fall at a slant toward you. Starlight does the same: because Earth is moving, every star looks tilted slightly in the direction we are heading, and the tilt reverses over the year as we circle the Sun. The tilt gives our speed, 30 km/s, exactly what it takes to go around the Sun once a year. If Earth stood still, distant stars would have to move faster than light to fake it.
